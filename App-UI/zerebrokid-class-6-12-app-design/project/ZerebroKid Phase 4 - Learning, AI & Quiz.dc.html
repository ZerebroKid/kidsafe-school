04 · SECTION 4 — CURIOSITY
Section 4 · Curiosity 1 / 6
Three questions worth asking
Why
Why must both sides change together?
3x + 3 balanced
3x tilted
Remove 3 from the left only and the two sides stop being equal. The equals sign is a promise.
Why call the smallest number x?
What if
What if the answer comes out as a decimal?
3x + 3 = 70 x = 22.33
Then no three consecutive whole numbers add to 70 — the maths is fine, the question is impossible. Spotting that is the real skill.
What if x turns out negative?
Now what
Where does this go next? Two unknowns instead of one — that's Class 9.

05 · SECTION 6 — COMMON MISTAKES
Section 6 · Common Mistakes 1 / 3
Everyone slips on these three. Spot them now, not in the exam.
Mistake 1 · dropping the +3
Often written 3x = 72 x = 24
Correct 3x + 3 = 72 x = 23
x+1 and x+2 carry a hidden +3. Write the brackets before you simplify.
Mistake 2 · answering the wrong question
Often written "x = 23" and stop
Correct "The integers are 23, 24, 25"
The question asked for three numbers. Finish the sentence and you keep the mark.
